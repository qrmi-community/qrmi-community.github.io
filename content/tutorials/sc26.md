Integrating quantum resources into HPC workflows is complicated by two layers of
fragmentation: quantum hardware vendors expose incompatible APIs, and HPC
centers run diverse job schedulers.

QRMI, an open-source project developed by a growing community of 15 national
laboratories, institutions and quantum vendors, addresses both by exposing
quantum resources as first-class schedulable resources alongside CPUs and GPUs,
independent of vendor or scheduler.

This hands-on tutorial has three modules, one per scheduling environment.
Participants submit real jobs to cloud quantum providers including IBM, Pasqal
and Alice & Bob.

## Learning Objectives

By the end of this tutorial, participants will be able to:

1. Explain why a quantum resource is not just another GPU, and what a batch scheduler needs to manage one.
2. Describe the architecture of QRMI (Quantum Resource Management Interface) and the acquire → run → release lifecycle of a QPU job.
3. Run QRMI-managed jobs on cloud-accessible quantum processing units (QPUs) from common HPC schedulers, including Slurm, PBS, IBM LSF, and Open Cluster Scheduler (hands-on).
4. Understand how QRMI integrates with Kubernetes and the Flux Framework, and the future directions of quantum-HPC integration.
5. Plan the first steps toward quantum-ready HPC at their own sites.

## Before the session

Bring a laptop with a container engine such as Docker or Podman, on Linux,
macOS, or Windows with WSL 2. Each workload manager runs as a small cluster of
containers; set up the containers before you arrive, as described in the
[tutorial materials](https://github.com/qrmi-community/SC26).

For the Pasqal exercises, create a free account on the
[Pasqal User Portal](https://portal.pasqal.cloud/) a few days in advance. The
Explorer offer includes the `EMU_FREE` emulator used in the hands-on and
requires no contract or payment.

For the IBM Quantum and Alice & Bob exercises, follow the account setup and onboarding steps in the [tutorial materials](https://github.com/qrmi-community/SC26).
